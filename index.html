Step 01 — Diode fundamentals

A diode conducts mainly in one direction. In forward bias it allows current after sufficient forward voltage; in reverse bias it approximately blocks current unless a breakdown mechanism operates (as in a Zener diode).

Diode modelON-state dropOFF state
Ideal diode0 V (short circuit)Open circuit, I = 0
Silicon (Si)Approximately 0.7 VOpen circuit
Germanium (Ge)Approximately 0.3 VOpen circuit
LEDUse the value given in the question, e.g. 2 VOpen circuit
VR = IR   ·   ΣV = 0 (KVL)   ·   ΣIin = ΣIout (KCL)
✦ THE MOST IMPORTANT HABIT

Guess whether a diode is ON or OFF. Replace it by its equivalent, solve the network, and then check that the assumed state is physically consistent. For a Si ON diode, use the 0.7 V approximation unless the question specifies ideal.

Step 02 — Load-line analysis

তোমার note-এর page 1 এবং book §2.2-এ supply, diode ও resistor দিয়ে Q-point বের করা হয়েছে। The Q-point is the intersection of the load line and diode characteristic.

E = VD + ID R   ⇒   ID = (E − VD) / R
Y-AXIS INTERCEPT

VD = 0

ID,max = E / R

X-AXIS INTERCEPT

ID = 0

VD,max = E

Conceptual load-line sketch. For an actual problem, use the given axes and diode characteristic.
WORKED EXAMPLE · note p. 1 / book Examples 2.1–2.3

Take E = 10 V and R = 0.5 kΩ. The load-line intercepts are:

1 ID,max = 10 / 500 = 20 mA.

2 VD,max = 10 V.

3 For a silicon approximate model, VD ≈ 0.7 V.

4 ID = (10 − 0.7) / 500 = 18.6 mA.

Q ≈ (VD = 0.7 V, ID = 18.6 mA)

Step 03 — Series diode circuits

Use the circuit's voltage sources and polarity to decide whether the diode conducts. In a simple forward-biased silicon diode circuit, subtract the approximate 0.7 V drop before finding the resistor current.

Forward biased Si series circuit — textbook Example 2.4 / lecture page 2
WORKED EXAMPLE · 8 V & 2.2 kΩ

For the conducting Si diode: VD ≈ 0.7 V, VR = 8 − 0.7 = 7.3 V.

I = 7.3 / 2200 = 0.003318 A.

I ≈ 3.32 mA   ·   VR = 7.3 V

Reverse the diode: it is OFF. Thus I = 0 and VR = 0; the entire applied voltage magnitude appears across the open diode.

Try the live diode calculator

Current ≈ 3.32 mA · Resistor voltage = 7.30 V

Two supplies and two resistors

I = (E1 + |E2| − 0.7) / (R1 + R2) for the teacher's indicated source polarities

In your note page 3, E1 = 10 V, E2 = −5 V, R1 = 4.7 kΩ and R2 = 2.2 kΩ. The current is 14.3 / 6.9 kΩ = 2.07 mA; the book's marked output polarity yields Vo ≈ −0.45 V.

Step 04 — Parallel & series-parallel diode

For parallel elements, voltage is the same. Apply KCL at the junction: Itotal = ID1 + ID2. When the textbook assumes two matching conducting diode branches, it divides current equally.

WORKED EXAMPLE · lecture p. 4, textbook Example 2.10

A 10 V supply feeds a 0.33 kΩ resistor and two identical, parallel forward-biased Si diodes.

Itotal = (10 − 0.7) / 0.33 kΩ = 28.18 mA.

Each matching diode: ID1 = ID2 = 28.18 / 2 = 14.09 mA.

Itotal = 28.18 mA   |   ID1 = ID2 ≈ 14.09 mA
WORKED EXAMPLE · LED resistor, lecture p. 4

Source = 8 V, green LED forward drop = 2 V, target LED current = 20 mA.

R = (8 − 2) / 0.020 = 300 Ω

Another branch in note p. 5 uses ID2 = I2 − I1 ≈ 3.32 − 0.212 = 3.11 mA.

✦ CAUTION

Two physical diodes in parallel do not necessarily split current exactly 50/50; the equal split above is a textbook assumption for matching diodes. Always follow the question's stated conditions.

Step 05 — Implementing AND & OR gates

In the teacher's page 6–7 examples: logic 0 = 0 V and logic 1 = +5 V. With real silicon diodes, the circuit's analog voltage can be approximately 0.7 V (LOW) or 4.3 V (HIGH), even though the logic interpretation is binary.

AND GATE

Output HIGH only when both inputs are HIGH

Diode cathodes face the input lines, their anodes meet at the output; a resistor pulls output toward +5 V.

OR GATE

Output HIGH when any input is HIGH

Diode anodes face the input lines, their cathodes join at the output; a resistor pulls output toward 0 V.

Conceptual arrangements. Check individual diode cathode bars in your teacher's actual circuit when determining the gate.

Gate identification trick: with one input LOW and one HIGH, does the output go LOW (AND) or HIGH (OR)? Re-create the gate's state table by reasoning about current paths; don't guess based on arrow direction alone.

Step 06 — Rectifiers & transformer

A rectifier converts alternating input into pulsating, unidirectional output. Sir covered the half-wave, full-wave bridge and center-tapped rectifiers on notebook pages 17–19.

TopologyIdeal average outputConductionPIV per diode
Half-wave (1 diode)Vm / π ≈ 0.318 VmOne half-cycleVm
Bridge full-wave (4 diodes)2Vm / π ≈ 0.636 VmTwo diodes on each half-cycleVm
Center-tapped (2 diodes)2Vm / π ≈ 0.636 VmOne diode on each half-cycle2Vm
✦ DEFINE Vm CAREFULLY

In a bridge, Vm is the peak across the entire secondary. In a center-tapped rectifier, Vm is the peak across one half of the secondary. Confusing them can double the calculated result.

Interactive waveform comparison

Input ACResulting output
Vpeak = √2 × Vrms   |   Vs,rms / Vp,rms = Ns / Np
WORKED EXAMPLE · 12 V RMS secondary

Input peak Vm = 12√2 ≈ 16.97 V.

Ideal half-wave average: 0.318 × 16.97 ≈ 5.40 V.

Ideal bridge full-wave average: 0.636 × 16.97 ≈ 10.80 V.

Half-wave PIV ≥ 16.97 V   |   Bridge PIV ≥ 16.97 V

A practical design should select a safely higher PIV rating. With Si diodes, the bridge has approximately two 0.7 V drops in its conducting path.

Center-tapped rectifier — how to write the answer

  1. The center tap divides the transformer secondary into two opposite-phase halves.
  2. In the positive half-cycle, D1 conducts and D2 blocks.
  3. In the negative half-cycle, D2 conducts and D1 blocks.
  4. Current through the load keeps the same direction; output is full-wave.
  5. Ideal average DC = 0.636 Vm, and PIV of each diode is 2Vm.

Step 07 — Clipper circuits

A clipper removes or limits selected portions of the input waveform. Sir taught positive and negative series, shunt, biased, and combined clippers on class-note pages 8–14. For every question, determine diode conduction separately over each interval.

SERIES CLIPPER

Diode in signal path

When the diode is OFF, load current is zero and output across the load resistor is commonly 0 V.

SHUNT CLIPPER

Diode across the load

When the diode conducts, it limits output near a reference / bias level.

POSITIVE CLIPPER

Clips positive portion

Leaves zero or a flat limit for part of the positive waveform.

NEGATIVE CLIPPER

Clips negative portion

Leaves zero or a flat limit for part of the negative waveform.

How to solve a biased clipper (five steps)

  1. Copy the diode bar direction, DC battery polarity and output terminals accurately.
  2. Decide ON/OFF for each possible voltage interval.
  3. With the diode ON, replace it by a short (ideal) or a 0.7 V drop (Si).
  4. Use KVL to find the transition point where it turns ON or OFF.
  5. Draw the full input waveform and plot the piecewise output against the same time axis.
WORKED EXAMPLE · Ideal two-sided clipping

If the circuit has an upper limiter of +3 V and a lower limiter of −2 V, output becomes:

vo = −2 V if vi < −2 V;
vo = vi if −2 ≤ vi ≤ +3;
vo = +3 V if vi > +3 V.

Draw a sine wave with its upper crest flattened at +3 V and its lower trough flattened at −2 V.

✦ WATCH THE DIODE DIRECTION

Never decide that a circuit clips the positive half-cycle merely because the diode symbol points left. Examine the actual DC polarity and where the output is measured. The waveform simulator above demonstrates idealized common cases, not every biased arrangement.

Step 08 — Clamper circuits

Unlike a clipper, a clamper shifts a waveform up or down without changing its peak-to-peak value under the ideal steady-state assumption. A basic clamper has a diode, series capacitor and resistor. Your note pages 15–16 use square-wave illustrations.

Vpp,out ≈ Vpp,in     and     RC ≫ signal period (during diode OFF)
DOWNWARD SHIFT

Upper level clamped at 0

For square input ±V, the resulting ideal output moves between 0 and −2V.

UPWARD SHIFT

Lower level clamped at 0

For square input ±V, the resulting ideal output moves between 0 and +2V.

Clamper-solving procedure

  1. Find the input half-cycle that forward-biases the diode.
  2. Assume the capacitor charges quickly while the diode is ON.
  3. Hold the capacitor voltage approximately constant in the OFF interval.
  4. Apply KVL and use the stored capacitor polarity to find output.
  5. For biased clampers, include the battery voltage and its sign.
WORKED EXAMPLE · Class-note square-wave model

Input switches between +5 V and −5 V. Peak-to-peak input is 10 V.

Downward clamp with maximum at 0 V: output 0 to −10 V.

Upward clamp with minimum at 0 V: output 0 to +10 V.

Both outputs keep Vpp = 10 V; only the DC level changes.

Step 09 — Zener diode & voltage regulation

A Zener diode is designed to operate in reverse breakdown so that the output remains approximately at its specified Zener voltage VZ while the device is correctly biased and remains within its current and power limits. Your note pp. 20–22 introduce three important numerical cases.

Basic Zener shunt regulator: series resistor feeds both the Zener and the parallel load.
IR = (Vi − VZ) / R   |   IL = VZ / RL   |   IZ = IR − IL

Case A — Fixed supply and fixed load

First remove the Zener and calculate the voltage divider output. This is your teacher's crucial ON/OFF test:

VL,open-test = Vi RL / (R + RL)
WORKED EXAMPLE · lecture p. 21

Take Vi = 16 V, R = 1 kΩ, VZ = 10 V.

A For RL = 1.2 kΩ, test VL = 16 × 1.2 / (1 + 1.2) = 8.73 V. Since 8.73 V < 10 V, Zener stays OFF.

B For RL = 3 kΩ, test VL = 16 × 3 / (1 + 3) = 12 V, exceeding 10 V, so Zener can regulate.

IR = (16 − 10)/1 kΩ = 6 mA ; IL = 10/3 kΩ = 3.33 mA.

VL = 10 V   |   IZ ≈ 2.67 mA (for RL = 3 kΩ)

Case B — Vi fixed; RL varies

At smaller load resistance, IL rises and Zener current falls. At larger load resistance, load current falls and Zener current rises. Use the boundary Zener current to find limiting loads.

RL,min ≈ VZ / (IR − IZ,min)   if the denominator is positive
IZ,max = PZ,max / VZ   |   RL,max = VZ / (IR − IZ,max) only when IR > IZ,max

If IR ≤ IZ,max, Zener power is not exceeded even at no load; there is no finite upper RL bound from that condition alone. Sir references textbook Example 2.27 for this case.

Case C — Vi varies; RL fixed

With the Zener regulated, calculate how the series current changes at the minimum and maximum supply voltages; check whether Zener current remains in its permitted range.

Vi,min = VZ + R (IL + IZ,min)
Vi,max = VZ + R (IL + IZ,max)

Your notebook marks this as Example 2.28 from the book. For design questions, a chosen series resistor must protect the Zener at the maximum input while still supplying the load at the minimum input.

Interactive Zener regulator calculator

Ideal fixed-voltage Zener model. Real regulation additionally requires the specified knee current and power rating.

✦ IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

A Zener with calculated IZ ≤ 0 is not operating as a regulator. In that case, solve the circuit using the resistor divider, rather than automatically setting output to VZ.

SOLUTION 01 · 3 MARKS

Assuming the Si diode conducts, VD = 0.7 V. KVL: E = VD + VR.

VR = 8 − 0.7 = 7.3 V.

I = VR/R = 7.3/2200 = 0.003318 A = 3.32 mA.

VD = 0.7 V, VR = 7.3 V, I ≈ 3.32 mA.

When reversed, diode is OFF: I = 0, VR = 0 and |VD| = 8 V.

SOLUTION 02 · 4 MARKS

(a) Parallel Si diodes each have ≈0.7 V forward drop. Source current I = (10 − 0.7)/330 = 28.18 mA.

With identical diodes and the textbook's equal-sharing assumption: ID1 = ID2 ≈ 14.09 mA.

(b) AND is HIGH only at inputs 1,1; OR is HIGH at 0,1 / 1,0 / 1,1. An AND gate's resistor pulls output upward; an OR gate's resistor pulls output downward.

Isource = 28.18 mA, each diode ≈14.09 mA (matching assumption).
SOLUTION 03 · 4 MARKS

Peak voltage Vm = √2 × 12 = 16.97 V.

Half-wave: VDC = Vm/π = 16.97/π = 5.40 V.

Bridge: VDC = 2Vm/π ≈ 10.80 V.

Bridge PIV = Vm of the complete secondary. Center-tapped PIV = 2Vm of each half-secondary.

16.97 V peak, 5.40 V half-wave, 10.80 V bridge.
SOLUTION 04 · 4 MARKS

A clipper cuts / limits a part of the waveform. A clamper shifts the entire waveform's DC level, approximately preserving peak-to-peak voltage.

Input is +5 V and −5 V, so Vpp = 10 V. Highest clamped output is 0 V.

Therefore output alternates between 0 V and −10 V.

Output levels: 0 V and −10 V; a square wave below the zero axis.
SOLUTION 05 · 5 MARKS

1 Remove Zener to test the divider. VL,open-test = 16 × 3 /(1 + 3) = 12 V.

2 Since 12 V > VZ = 10 V, the Zener enters regulation in the ideal model.

3 VL = 10 V and IR = (16 − 10)/1000 = 6 mA.

4 IL = 10/3000 = 3.33 mA.

5 IZ = IR − IL = 6 − 3.33 ≈ 2.67 mA.

Zener ON · VL = 10 V · IR = 6 mA · IL = 3.33 mA · IZ = 2.67 mA

Actual regulation also depends on the minimum knee current and Zener power rating.
